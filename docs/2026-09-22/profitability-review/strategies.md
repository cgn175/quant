# Strategy edge review — own capital below USD 1,000

Scope: read-only audit of the Go strategies, Python research scripts, configs and stored research artifacts in this repository. The target is proprietary trading with less than USD 1,000. This is an economic and implementation review; it is not a live-trading recommendation and it does not prove a positive edge.

The repository contains enough infrastructure to run several ideas, but it does not yet contain a trustworthy, cost-complete performance proof for any of them. The ranking below is therefore a ranking of what is worth validating next, not a claim that the current implementation is profitable.

## Ranking for a sub-USD-1,000 account

| Rank | Strategy | Fit for the capital | Current economic verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (conditional) | Delta-neutral funding carry | Potentially the best small-account candidate after resizing and accounting repairs | The carry idea is understandable, but the current implementation overstates collected funding, defaults to a USD 1,000 position, and is directional when the example config disables the spot hedge. Paper results cannot be trusted until settlement-time accounting is fixed. |
| 2 (research only) | Trend following | Capital-efficient; one leg per trade and risk-based sizing | The system can trade it with small capital, but the live Go entry is materially simpler than the advertised Plan D and the repository itself records negative IC for the configured plan. It is the best candidate for a clean re-validation, not for immediate deployment. |
| 3 (research only) | Mean reversion | Capital-efficient in principle | There is no bot/config route for it. The signal has an OR trigger, a weak-history bypass, and a 1.5 ATR target against a 2 ATR stop; after realistic costs the required win rate is high. |
| 4 | Basis trade | Poor fit below USD 1,000 | The example requires USD 1,000 per position and two legs. The annualization treats a spot-perpetual price gap like a repeatedly compounding 8-hour return, so the 15% threshold corresponds to only about 1.28 bps of instantaneous basis. That is below a realistic four-leg round-trip cost. |
| 5 | Market making | Poor fit until execution and inventory controls are repaired | The quoted 0.1% half-spread can cover configured fees on paper, but the strategy has no equity-linked inventory cap, refreshes/cancels every second, and the paper fill model has no queue, latency, partial fills or adverse selection. The round-trip recording path also sends a new unfilled limit order to the execution engine. |
| 6 | Liquidation cascade | Not tradable in the current bot | The bot emits metrics and logs a signal; it never opens a position. The long/short ratio and liquidation cluster are estimates derived from funding and fixed leverage assumptions rather than observed liquidation levels. |
| 7 | Cross-sectional momentum filter | Not a standalone strategy and not integrated | The scorer exists, but trend `OnBar` explicitly says the filter is disabled. The separate Python comparison has no fees or slippage and uses a simplified 3% stop/opposite-signal model, so its claimed improvement is a research hypothesis only. |

The practical decision is: keep the account in paper mode; repair and test funding carry and trend separately; do not allocate capital to basis, MM, liquidation or momentum until their blockers below are closed.

## Capital and cost arithmetic

The example funding config sets `position_size_usd: 1000`, `min_funding_rate: 0.0001`, `exit_threshold: 0.00005`, `scan_interval_ms: 300000`, and `delta_neutral: false` ([config.example.funding.yaml:15-23](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/config.example.funding.yaml)). The basis example also sets `position_size_usd: 1000` ([config.example.basis.yaml:15-28](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/config.example.basis.yaml)). A delta-neutral trade of USD 1,000 per leg has USD 2,000 gross exposure. Required cash is spot purchase plus futures margin plus reserves/fees; gross exposure must not be added to margin as if it were all cash. The USD 1,000 spot purchase alone already exhausts an account below USD 1,000.

The repository's fee setting is a percentage, with `0.1` meaning 0.1% per side and 0.1% as the default ([internal/config/config.go:96-103](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/config/config.go:96), [internal/config/config.go:520-522](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/config/config.go:520)). The example files use 0.04% and 5 bps slippage ([config.example.trend.yaml:48-52](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/config.example.trend.yaml:48), [config.example.funding.yaml:45-47](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/config.example.funding.yaml:45), [config.example.basis.yaml:37-39](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/config.example.basis.yaml:37), [config.example.mm.yaml:52-54](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/config.example.mm.yaml:52)). Using those as a sensitivity range:

* One directional round trip costs approximately `2 × (4 bps fee + 5 bps slippage) = 18 bps`, or USD 1.80 on USD 1,000 notional. At the default 10 bps fee, it is about 30 bps, or USD 3.00.
* A spot-plus-perp delta-neutral round trip has four legs: approximately 36 bps, or USD 3.60 on USD 1,000, before basis movement, funding variation, borrow/margin effects and failed-leg risk. At the default fee it is about 60 bps, or USD 6.00.
* At the funding entry threshold of 1 bp per 8 hours, gross carry is only USD 0.10 per USD 1,000 notional per payment. The 36 bps four-leg cost needs about 36 payments, roughly 12 days at three payments per day, merely to break even if the rate stays at the threshold. The rate is not guaranteed to persist.
* The basis code's 15% annualized threshold implies `basis = 1.15^(1/1095)-1 = 0.01276%`, about 1.28 bps, if interpreted using its own compounding formula. A one-time 1.28 bps convergence on USD 1,000 is USD 0.13, far below the illustrative USD 3.60 four-leg cost. This is a threshold arithmetic result, not a claim about exchange fees.
* Mean reversion's default target is 1.5 ATR against a 2 ATR stop ([internal/strategy/mean_reversion.go:652-665](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/mean_reversion.go:652)). At 0.5% risk on USD 1,000, an illustrative 1% ATR gives USD 250 notional and USD 5 risk. With 18 bps round-trip cost, a win is about USD 3.30 net and a loss about USD 5.45 net; the break-even win rate is about 62.3%. At 30 bps costs it is about 65.7%. The required rate is above the configured 60% recent-history gate, before model error and same-bar execution ambiguity.

These are hurdle calculations. The actual test must use the account's fee tier, maker/taker mix, spread, quantity rounding, minimum notional, funding settlement timestamps and slippage distribution.

## Trend following: capital-efficient but not yet demonstrated

The strategy's declared configuration includes EMA(9/21) confirmation, EMA(50), volume and other filters ([internal/strategy/trend.go:29-116](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:29)). The actual Go `OnBar` implementation only applies the shifted Donchian breakout and close-versus-EMA(50) direction check ([internal/strategy/trend.go:592-619](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:592)). The same function then contains an explicit TODO saying the cross-sectional momentum filter is disabled ([internal/strategy/trend.go:633-638](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:633)). Therefore documentation describing EMA crossover, volume confirmation and momentum as active does not describe the current Go path.

The active baseline is still economically coherent: ADX fallback blocks weak trends ([internal/strategy/trend.go:846-855](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:846), ATR(14)/ATR(50) bounds volatility ([internal/strategy/trend.go:857-864](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:857), and funding blocks crowded direction or halves size ([internal/strategy/trend.go:866-880](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:866)). ATR sizing uses equity risk and a leverage cap ([internal/strategy/trend.go:1301-1334](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:1301), and stops are tightened only on closed bars while stop-hit checks run every tick ([internal/strategy/trend.go:975-1089](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:975)). Those are useful risk controls, but they do not create alpha by themselves.

There are two economic concerns in the exit design. Partial exits are only 10% at 3R and 10% at 6R by the Go defaults ([internal/strategy/trend.go:169-180](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:169)), so most of the position still depends on the trailing stop. The dynamic ATR multiplier returns 2.0 for both `R > 6` and `R > 4` ([internal/strategy/trend.go:1112-1121](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:1112)), so the advertised multi-stage tightening has no separate `>6R` stage.

The repository's own status document says the configured Plan D had mean IC `-0.044` ([docs/STRATEGY_STATUS.md:65-66](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/docs/STRATEGY_STATUS.md:65)). A historical filter-ablation artifact reports Sharpe 1.45 for all filters on 427 trades, but also reports that the EMA crossover alone reduced Sharpe from 1.02 to 0.35 ([results/filter_ablation_1771110472.txt:11-32](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/results/filter_ablation_1771110472.txt:11)). These artifacts are useful warnings, not proof: they may represent a Python signal path different from the current Go path, and the test protocol, costs and multiple-testing history must be audited before using the numbers.

Another stored walk-forward artifact reports 213 trades, average return -0.4358% per trade, total return -92.8%, Sharpe -2.47 and profit factor 0.659 (`results/walk_forward_summary.json`). Its companion script is a regime-aware meta-labeling experiment rather than the exact current Go strategy, so it should not be presented as a direct Plan D result; it is still a strong reason to demand a fresh, production-parity holdout.

Verdict: trend is the cleanest strategy to revalidate at USD 500–1,000 because it does not require two simultaneous legs and its risk size scales down. It is not competitive enough to fund until the Go/Python signal parity is fixed and a fresh walk-forward test shows positive net expectancy after 0.1% per-side fee and slippage sensitivity.

## Funding arbitrage: promising idea, invalid current accounting

The strategy enters when absolute funding clears the threshold, takes the opposite perp side, and optionally buys or sells spot as a hedge ([internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:253-360](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:253)). The directional version explicitly skips the price-loss guard only when a spot hedge exists; with no hedge, a 3% adverse move can trigger the max-loss close ([internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:439-452](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:439)). This means the default example is carry plus directional beta, not arbitrage.

The largest blocker is the funding-payment estimator. The 5-minute scan loop is configured in the example and used directly by `runLoop` ([internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:169-188](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:169)). On every management pass, the code adds `currentFunding × size × markPrice` to `FundingCollected` and increments `FundingPayments` ([internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:408-423](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:408)). The comment itself calls this a simplified assumption that payment was received since the last check. It is not keyed to a funding event or settlement timestamp. At an 8-hour schedule, a 5-minute loop can count roughly 96 synthetic payments for one actual period; the exact factor depends on the venue's current schedule. Funding snapshots are also stored every scan ([internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:219-239](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:219), [internal/data/funding_store.go:157-183](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/data/funding_store.go:157)), so the 24-hour momentum average is an average of repeated observations rather than distinct funding events.

The strategy also executes spot hedge orders directly through the executor ([internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:337-360](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:337), [internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:514-526](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:514)). The execution engine's fee/PnL accounting is attached to the perp close path ([internal/execution/engine.go:194-247](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/execution/engine.go:194)), so direct spot-leg fees are absent from the strategy's reported total unless the exchange's external account report is used. This makes paper PnL and logs optimistic or incomplete.

The momentum variant is not evidence of an edge yet. Its rule is simply `abs(current) > threshold` and `abs(current) > abs(avg24h) × multiplier` ([internal/strategy/funding_arb/momentum.go:9-33](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/momentum.go:9)). The repository comment claims 30–40% improvement, but this needs a source and a cost-complete, event-level test.

Cross-exchange funding arbitrage is currently a scanner stub. It creates an in-memory position and registers exposure after logging “execution not implemented”; no orders are placed ([internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:614-647](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:614)). Its close path is also explicitly TODO ([internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:683-705](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/strategy.go:683)). The scanner calculates transfer cost but sets `netAnnualizedReturn` equal to gross annualized return ([internal/strategy/funding_arb/cross_exchange.go:130-153](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/funding_arb/cross_exchange.go:130)), so opportunity logs are not net opportunities.

Verdict: after fixing settlement accounting, actual two-leg/spot-leg fees, hedge failure handling and size scaling, delta-neutral funding carry is the most plausible small-account research candidate. Until then it should be treated as an accounting demo, not a competitive return engine.

## Basis trade: threshold and capital mismatch

The basis strategy computes `(markPrice - spotPrice) / spotPrice`, then annualizes it as `(1 + basis)^(365×3) - 1` ([internal/strategy/basis_trade/strategy.go:175-204](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/basis_trade/strategy.go:175)). A perpetual contract has no fixed maturity at which the price gap must converge. The current price premium is not itself a return that compounds every funding interval. Consequently, the entry threshold is not a reliable expected return estimate. The same strategy holds until the basis falls below the exit threshold, with no maximum holding time or carry forecast ([internal/strategy/basis_trade/strategy.go:310-325](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/basis_trade/strategy.go:310)).

Entry buys spot and shorts perp using the same requested base size ([internal/strategy/basis_trade/strategy.go:216-270](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/basis_trade/strategy.go:216)). It logs spot-plus-perp price PnL but does not add funding payments to the basis position ([internal/strategy/basis_trade/strategy.go:350-369](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/basis_trade/strategy.go:350)). As with funding arb, spot orders bypass the engine's per-leg fee accounting. For a USD 1,000 account, the configured USD 1,000 position is not feasible once both legs and margin are considered.

The cross-exchange basis manager uses a different 90-day linear annualization formula ([internal/strategy/basis_trade/cross_exchange.go:74-110](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/basis_trade/cross_exchange.go:74)), while the main strategy's scan loop always fetches one perp mark and one spot price and does not branch into the manager ([internal/strategy/basis_trade/strategy.go:175-213](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/basis_trade/strategy.go:175)). That is a code/design parity problem even before profitability.

Verdict: do not run this with own capital below USD 1,000. If retained, replace the annualization with a measured convergence/holding-time model, include realized funding and all four legs' costs, and reject any entry whose expected convergence PnL does not exceed a conservative cost and slippage hurdle.

## Market making: gross spread is not net edge

The example quotes a 0.1% spread on each side, order amount 0.001 BTC, one-second refresh, and max inventory 1 BTC ([config.example.mm.yaml:15-40](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/config.example.mm.yaml:15)). The actual quote width is two times `spread` around the reservation price ([internal/strategy/market_making/strategy.go:467-571](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/market_making/strategy.go:467)). At 4 bps fee per side, a buy-then-sell pair has 20 bps gross spread minus 8 bps fees, before adverse selection, inventory mark-to-market and missed/partial fills. At the default 10 bps fee, the quoted spread is already consumed by fees. This is why the exchange fee tier and maker rebate must be a first-class input to any MM test.

There is no equity-based inventory limit. The code only stops one side when base-asset inventory exceeds `MaxInventory` ([internal/strategy/market_making/strategy.go:542-571](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/market_making/strategy.go:542)); on a USD 1,000 account, `max_inventory: 1.0` BTC could represent many times the account equity. The one-second loop cancels existing orders and recreates them ([internal/strategy/market_making/strategy.go:397-409](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/market_making/strategy.go:397), [internal/strategy/market_making/strategy.go:454-465](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/market_making/strategy.go:454)), destroying queue priority and making real fills very different from the paper path.

The paper executor fills a limit order whenever the current market price crosses its limit, at the full size and posted price, with no queue, depth, latency or partial-fill model ([internal/execution/paper.go:92-128](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/execution/paper.go:92)). That systematically makes passive strategies look safer than they are. The MM strategy then records a round trip by calling `ClosePosition`, which submits another limit order even though the sell order has already filled ([internal/strategy/market_making/strategy.go:698-717](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/market_making/strategy.go:698)). `ClosePosition` immediately computes PnL from the new order's `FilledPrice` ([internal/execution/engine.go:194-247](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/execution/engine.go:194)), which is zero for a new paper limit order. This makes MM trade accounting invalid until repaired.

The config leaves imbalance disabled ([config.example.mm.yaml:43-50](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/config.example.mm.yaml:43), and order-flow is disabled by default ([internal/config/config.go:254-263](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/config/config.go:254)). Thus the current configuration has no measured adverse-selection edge beyond a static/dynamic spread.

Verdict: no live capital. First repair round-trip accounting, cap inventory by equity and stress fills with L2 replay/queue position. The strategy can become a small-account experiment on a very liquid pair, but the current paper PnL is not evidence.

## Mean reversion: not an executable product slice

The repository has a substantial `MeanReversionStrategy`, but the top-level bot dispatcher has cases for trend, MM, funding, basis and liquidation only ([cmd/bot/main.go:50-64](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/cmd/bot/main.go:50)). There is no mean-reversion strategy config in `StrategyConfig`; the implementation is currently library/tests and a standalone research script.

The Go entry accepts any one of RSI extreme, Bollinger excursion or MA deviation because the conditions are joined with OR ([internal/strategy/mean_reversion.go:512-574](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/mean_reversion.go:512)). It then blocks ADX above 25, requires ATR% between 0.5% and 3%, checks recent reversion history and applies funding direction filters ([internal/strategy/mean_reversion.go:601-647](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/mean_reversion.go:601)). The history test returns true when fewer than five qualifying observations exist ([internal/strategy/mean_reversion.go:700-755](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/mean_reversion.go:700)), so its 60% success requirement is bypassed exactly when evidence is sparse.

The target/stop asymmetry and cost hurdle are unfavorable for a small account unless the signal has a very high conditional win rate. The code exits on the stop before checking the target within a candle ([internal/strategy/mean_reversion.go:795-815](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/mean_reversion.go:795)); the research script and any future backtest must specify the same-bar ordering rule. The standalone backtest uses fees/slippage, but its symbol list explicitly keeps only symbols that performed well and removes weak symbols ([scripts/backtest_mean_reversion.py:28-37](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/scripts/backtest_mean_reversion.py:28), a survivorship/selection warning).

Verdict: keep as a research branch. Integrate only after a pre-registered, point-in-time, cost-complete test across all available symbols, including the removed names and a walk-forward holdout.

## Liquidation cascade: signal-only and model-based clusters

The detector requires funding, 24-hour OI change and an estimated long/short ratio ([internal/strategy/liquidation/strategy.go:96-129](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/liquidation/strategy.go:96)). Its long/short ratio is explicitly estimated from funding rather than fetched from the exchange ([internal/strategy/liquidation/strategy.go:154-178](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/liquidation/strategy.go:154)), and its liquidation cluster assumes 10x or 15x average leverage ([internal/strategy/liquidation/strategy.go:132-152](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/liquidation/strategy.go:132). The bot only updates Prometheus and logs high-confidence signals ([internal/bot/liquidation.go:95-135](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/bot/liquidation.go:95)); there is no order path or position/risk lifecycle.

The status document confirms that real liquidation heatmap data is missing and clusters are estimated ([docs/STRATEGY_STATUS.md:190-199](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/docs/STRATEGY_STATUS.md:190)).

Verdict: no capital allocation and no return ranking until observed liquidation events, a trade trigger, execution and exits exist.

## Momentum: useful hypothesis, not yet part of the bot

The Go scorer uses a 21-day, 4-hour-candle lookback and volatility-adjusted weighted returns ([internal/strategy/trend_momentum.go:17-63](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend_momentum.go:17)). However, trend `OnBar` says the multi-symbol filter is disabled ([internal/strategy/trend.go:633-638](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/internal/strategy/trend.go:633)). The separate Python momentum backtest updates equity by `pnl_pct × 1%`, but contains no commission, spread or slippage deduction ([scripts/backtest_momentum.py:84-165](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/scripts/backtest_momentum.py:84)). The historical report therefore cannot support the claim that the filter is production-ready ([docs/2026-02-19/PHASE_2_MOMENTUM_RESULTS.md:31-65](/home/hoangt/projects/quant/docs/2026-02-19/PHASE_2_MOMENTUM_RESULTS.md:31)).

Verdict: integrate only as a pre-registered ablation inside the actual Go trend pipeline, with costs and turnover included. Do not market it as a separate source of alpha.

## Prioritized validation plan

### P0 — make the PnL and execution state truthful

1. Funding: store the exchange's funding event time/id and count each event once; separate “current quoted rate” from “realized payment.” Base the momentum average on distinct settlement events.
2. Funding/Basis: model spot-leg fees, perp-leg fees, slippage, funding received/paid, hedge imbalance, margin usage, and failed-leg recovery. Use actual filled sizes rather than requested sizes.
3. Cross-exchange: do not create a position or register exposure before both legs are actually acknowledged; remove the execution-not-implemented path from any production mode.
4. MM: record an already completed buy/sell pair directly, or wait for a real close fill; do not submit a second unfilled limit order to calculate PnL. Add equity-linked gross and net inventory caps.
5. All strategies: enforce minimum notional/quantity/precision, reject a USD 1,000 position when available capital is below the required two-leg notional, and keep `mode: paper`.

### P1 — cost-complete research

* Use next-bar or next-tick execution after a signal; never fill at the same close used to generate it.
* Test fee tiers at 4, 10 and 20 bps per side, slippage at 5, 10 and 20 bps, spread crossing, funding settlement, quantity rounding, and partial fills.
* Report trade count, turnover, net expectancy, profit factor, max drawdown, time in market, funding/fee/slippage decomposition, and bootstrap confidence intervals. Use 100 completed trades as an initial review target, not proof of significance; effective sample size depends on dependence, regimes and return variance. Report uncertainty even when that count is exceeded.
* Split by bull, bear, high-volatility and range regimes. Require positive net expectancy in a held-out period and stability under ±20% parameter perturbations. Track every tested strategy/parameter variant to control multiple-testing bias.

### P2 — strategy-specific experiments

* Trend: compare the current Go path with a parity version that adds EMA crossover and volume; run filter ablations before enabling anything. The historical artifact says EMA crossover alone hurt, so it must be tested rather than assumed beneficial.
* Funding: use event-level historical funding, test static threshold versus momentum, and compare directional versus delta-neutral at USD 250, 500 and 750 per leg. The primary output should be net PnL per unit of collateral and worst mark-to-market loss, not APY from a snapshot rate.
* Basis: replace fixed compounding annualization with realized basis-change and holding-time labels. Require expected convergence PnL plus expected funding to exceed all four legs' costs at the chosen account size.
* MM: replay L2/order-flow data with queue position, latency, cancellation, partial fills and mark-to-market inventory. Report maker/taker mix and adverse-selection loss after each fill.
* Mean reversion: test OR versus confluence entries, remove the sparse-history bypass, fix same-bar stop/target ordering, and include all symbols rather than the selected “good” list.
* Liquidation: collect actual liquidation events and OI-by-price or a defensible proxy, then add an executable entry/exit/risk path before any performance test.

## Stop/go rules for this account size

The following are proposed screening thresholds, not validated guarantees or automatic live-trading authorization. Do not consider moving from paper to live unless the exact production code passes all of these after costs: positive out-of-sample expectancy, profit factor above 1.15, Sharpe above 0.8, max drawdown below 15%, positive windows in at least 60% of walk-forward periods, no unbounded inventory or directional hedge exposure, and no synthetic/fake fills or funding payments. If a strategy only passes before fees or only on selected surviving symbols, classify it as failed for deployment.

Claims that require external verification before being used in the main report include the exchange's current funding schedule, fee tiers/rebates, minimum order constraints, competitor performance/capacity, and any APY/Sharpe claims in repository documents. The repository comments describing “30–40%” momentum improvement, “20–30%” MM improvement, or annual targets are hypotheses, not proofs.
